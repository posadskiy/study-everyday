# Nutrition

> Current goal: lose weight slowly from **81 kg** while staying healthy for running. Eat breakfast + lunch every day; add post-run recovery only on run days. No routine food after 19:00.

## Meals

### Breakfast

**Target: 800–900 kcal, protein + carbs.**

- Greek yogurt + oats + banana + honey
- Eggs + bread + fruit
- Cottage cheese + oats/bread + fruit
- Whey + oats + banana

### Lunch

**Target: 1000–1200 kcal, protein + carbs.**

- Chicken/fish/beef + rice/potatoes/pasta + vegetables
- Eggs + bread/potatoes + vegetables

On run days: keep lunch lower fat and lower fiber so it digests before the 16:00 run.

### Post-run recovery (weekday)

**Within 30 minutes:** 30–40 g protein + 50–70 g carbs.

- Whey + banana + bread/jam
- Greek yogurt + banana + honey
- Cottage cheese + rice cakes + jam
- Eggs on toast

If the run finishes after 18:00, still take this. It is recovery food, not a snack.

## Saturday Long Run

A normal big breakfast is too heavy before a 9:00 run. Eat light before, make up calories after.

**Light breakfast options:** banana + rice cakes + honey, toast + jam, small oats + banana, small yogurt + banana.

**Avoid before the run:** eggs with cheese, fried food, nuts/oil-heavy meals, high-fiber bread, very large coffee without food.

**What to carry:** 2 × 500 ml flasks, 2 gels or 10–12 dates (eat at 40 min, then at 80 min), electrolyte in one flask if run is over 90 min or warm.

## Strength Before Breakfast

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|------------|
| Feel fine fasted | Train, then eat normal breakfast |
| Feel weak/dizzy | Banana or yogurt before; count as part of breakfast |
| Strength + run day | Breakfast after strength, run recovery after afternoon run |

Optional tendon support: **15 g collagen + 50 mg vitamin C** 30–60 min before strength.

## On-Run Fueling

### When to eat

| Run duration | Action |
|-------------|--------|
| Under 60 min | Water only |
| 60–90 min | 1 gel or dates, optional |
| 90 min – 3 hours | Fuel every 30–45 min — required |
| 3+ hours | Fuel every 30 min; mix gels + real food |

**Eat before hungry. Drink before thirsty.** Hunger lags 15–30 min behind actual need.

### Carbs per hour

| Duration | Target |
|----------|--------|
| 60–90 min | 30–60 g/hr |
| 90 min – 2.5 hours | 60 g/hr |
| 2.5+ hours | 60–90 g/hr |

Over 60 g/hr from one source, gut absorption stalls. Over 90 g/hr you need mixed carbs (glucose + fructose). Start at 30 g/hr and train the gut up over months.

### Fuel options

| Food | Carbs | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| Dates / dried figs | ~20 g per 30 g | Real food, cheaper, works well |
| Energy gel (Maurten, SiS, Gu) | 21–25 g | Fast, easy to carry. Test in training first. |
| Banana | ~25 g | Good at aid stations |
| Boiled potato + salt | ~15 g per 60 g | Best tolerated late in a long run |

Never try a new product on race day.

## Hydration

### What to carry per run

| Run | Carry | Electrolytes? |
|-----|-------|---------------|
| Under 45 min | Usually nothing | No |
| 45–75 min, cool | 0–500 ml water | No |
| 45–75 min, hot/sweaty | 500 ml water | Optional: pinch of salt |
| 75–120 min | 500 ml–1 L | Optional cool; yes if hot |
| 2–2.5 hours | 2 × 500 ml | One flask with salt/electrolyte |
| 2.5+ hours / hot Karst | 1–1.5 L | Electrolytes throughout |

**Weekday runs:** 0–500 ml plain water. No electrolytes needed unless hot or you see salt marks on skin/clothes.

### Daily drinking

| Day | Target |
|-----|--------|
| Rest day | 1.8–2.5 L total |
| Weekday run | 2.2–3.0 L total (includes during/after run) |
| Saturday long run | 2.5–4.0 L total |
| Hot Karst day | Add 0.5–1.0 L |

Drink to thirst. **Urine color:** pale yellow = good, dark yellow = drink more, completely clear = probably too much.

### How electrolytes work

Sweat loses water and sodium. Sodium keeps fluid in the blood and protects against cramps and hyponatremia (dangerously low sodium from drinking too much plain water on long runs).

You only need to actively plan for sodium. Potassium and magnesium come from normal food.

### Natural sodium sources (no tabs needed for normal training)

| Option | Sodium |
|--------|--------|
| 1/8 tsp table salt in 500 ml water | ~300 mg |
| Water + salt + honey + lemon (DIY drink) | ~300 mg per 500 ml |
| Salted potatoes / rice | Easy after runs |
| Soup or broth | Good after long/cold runs |
| Olives, pickles | Strong sodium, good post-run |
| Salted crackers / pretzels | Sodium + carbs during long runs |

Add electrolytes during runs over 90 min or in hot weather (target ~400–600 mg sodium per litre). After shorter runs, salted food at lunch is enough.

**Signs of sodium deficiency:** swelling in hands/feet, nausea, headache, confusion (stop immediately).

### Karst / summer

Karst is exposed, no shade. July–August can hit 35°C+. Bora wind accelerates sweat loss — you lose more than you feel.

- Long runs in summer: start before 8:00 or after 19:00
- Know where springs are on regular routes
- Carry 1.0–1.5 L for 2–3+ hour runs

## Weight Loss Strategy

**Current: 81 kg / 178 cm.** BMI is technically overweight but not the right tool for athletic people — you already finished a 32 km trail race. Weight affects performance mainly on uphills: every extra kg costs roughly 2–4% more energy per metre climbed.

**Target: 74–76 kg.** 5–7 kg from now, at safe rate takes 3–5 months.

| Range | What it means |
|-------|---------------|
| 80–85 kg | Now — functional, uphills harder than needed |
| **74–76 kg** | **First target — noticeable uphill improvement** |
| 70–74 kg | Lean amateur runner — realistic after 2–3 years |

**The rule:** never create a deficit on hard training days or long run day. Deficit on rest days and easy run days only.

### Macros — adjust as weight drops

| Weight | Protein (2.0–2.2 g/kg) | Carbs training day (4–5 g/kg) | Carbs rest day (3–4 g/kg) | Fat (0.8–1.0 g/kg) |
|--------|------------------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **81 kg (now)** | **162–178 g** | **324–405 g** | **243–324 g** | **65–81 g** |
| ~78 kg | 156–172 g | 312–390 g | 234–312 g | 62–78 g |
| ~75 kg | 150–165 g | 300–375 g | 225–300 g | 60–75 g |

Recalculate when weight drops ~3 kg. Track protein closely; carbs are flexible.

### Timeline

| Phase | Duration | Weight |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Now | — | 81 kg |
| Early progress | 2–3 months | 78–79 kg |
| First target | 4–6 months | 74–76 kg |
| Lean stable | 12–18 months training | 70–74 kg |

When you reach 74–76 kg: switch calorie targets to maintenance (~2700–3000 kcal on run days). Recalculate macros at new body weight.

## Supplements

| Supplement | Dose | When |
|------------|------|------|
| Creatine monohydrate | **5 g daily** | Run days: with post-run recovery. Rest days: with breakfast or lunch. |
| Collagen + Vit C | 15 g + 50 mg | 30–60 min before strength or hill workouts |
| Vitamin D | Test first | With a fatty meal if low. Important for bone adaptation. |

Creatine may increase scale weight by **1–2 kg** in the first 2–3 weeks — this is water inside muscle cells, not fat.
