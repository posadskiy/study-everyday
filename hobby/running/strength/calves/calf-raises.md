# Calf Raises (Loaded) — Straight Knee, Standing

Used in: **Session A (Tuesday)**. In progression plans this is **single-leg calf raise (straight knee)** off a step. From Month 2, pair with [seated-calf-raise-bent-knee.md](seated-calf-raise-bent-knee.md) in the same session. See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for full program.

**What it trains:** gastrocnemius and soleus (calf complex), Achilles tendon loading. Essential for preventing Achilles tendinopathy — one of the most common trail running injuries.

## How to do it
1. Stand on the edge of a step or stair with heels hanging off
2. Start at the bottom: heels below step level (full stretch)
3. Rise up onto toes over 1–2 seconds
4. Lower slowly back to the bottom over **3–4 seconds** (eccentric phase — this is where the training happens)
5. Full range: from full stretch at bottom to full toe raise at top

**Start with both legs. Progress to single-leg when 3×15 becomes easy.**

## Common mistakes
- Not going through full range — partial reps miss the tendon loading at the bottom
- Rushing the lowering phase — the slow downward movement is the most important part
- Locking the knee — keep a very slight bend throughout
- Holding the rail constantly — use it only for balance, not to offload weight

## Easier (beginner)
- Both feet on the step
- Shorter eccentric (2 seconds lowering)
- Smaller range of motion — build to full heel drop gradually
- Flat ground instead of a step (no hang at the bottom)

## Harder (progression)
- Single-leg (most important progression)
- Add weight: hold a dumbbell in one hand, or wear a backpack
- Slow the eccentric to 5–6 seconds
- Pause 2 seconds at the bottom stretch before rising
