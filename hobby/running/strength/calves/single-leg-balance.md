# Single-Leg Balance with Movement

Used in: **Session A (Tuesday)**. See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for full program.

**What it trains:** proprioception — the ankle and knee's automatic stabilization system. Weak proprioception is the main reason for ankle sprains on uneven terrain.

## How to do it (progressions in order)

**Level 1:** Stand on one foot for 30–60 seconds. Focus on keeping the ankle still. Switch sides.

**Level 2:** Close your eyes while balancing. Removes visual feedback and forces the ankle to work much harder.

**Level 3:** Stand on a folded mat, cushion, or foam pad (unstable surface) with eyes open, then closed.

**Level 4:** Catch and throw a ball while balancing, or trace alphabet letters in the air with the raised foot.

**Level 5:** Single-leg balance on trail terrain (a rock, a log) during training runs.

## Common mistakes
- Gripping the floor with toes — relax the foot
- Letting the standing ankle wobble wildly without trying to control it — actively try to stabilize
- Skipping progressions — Level 2 (eyes closed) is much harder than it looks and should be mastered before Level 3

## Easier (beginner)
- Stand near a wall with fingertips touching it lightly for safety
- Start with 15–20 seconds and build

## Harder (progression)
- Add dynamic movements: small hops and land on one foot, hold
- Perform a single-leg Romanian deadlift while balancing (combines proprioception with posterior chain loading)
- Run on progressively more technical trail terrain
