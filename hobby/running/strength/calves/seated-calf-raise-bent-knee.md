# Single-Leg Calf Raise (Bent Knee, Seated)

Used in: **Session A (Tuesday)** from Month 2 onward. See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for full program. Pair with [calf-raises.md](calf-raises.md) (straight knee, standing) in the same session.

**What it trains:** soleus — the deeper calf muscle that works with the knee bent. Trail running loads the soleus heavily on uphills and long flat efforts. Strong soleus supports Achilles health and reduces the “clogged calf” feeling when volume increases.

**Why seated and bent knee:** with the knee flexed, the gastrocnemius (straight-knee calf muscle) is shortened and contributes less. The soleus does most of the work — the same pattern as running with a bent knee.

## How to do it
1. Sit on a bench or sturdy chair, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart
2. Bend both knees to about **90 degrees** — thighs roughly parallel to the floor
3. Place the ball of one foot on a small block, weight plate, or the edge of a step (heel free to move). The other foot rests on the floor or is lifted slightly out of the way
4. Let the heel drop below the block level for a full stretch (2–3 cm is enough if you have no block — use floor only at first)
5. Rise onto the ball of the foot over **3 seconds**, squeezing at the top for 1 second
6. Lower the heel slowly over **3 seconds** to full stretch
7. Complete all reps on one side, then switch

**Tempo (Months 2–3):** 3 s up, 3 s down. **Months 4–5:** add weight on the working thigh; slow to 4 s up, 4 s down if programmed.

## Common mistakes
- Knee not bent enough — if the knee is straighter, the gastrocnemius takes over and you duplicate the standing calf raise
- Bouncing at the bottom — control the stretch; no rebound
- Rushing the lowering phase — eccentric load on the soleus and Achilles is the main training effect
- Pushing off the non-working leg — only the working foot moves
- Toes turned out — keep the foot pointing straight ahead

## Easier (beginner)
- Both feet on the floor, no block — smaller range, build heel drop over weeks
- Shorter tempo (2 s up, 2 s down) until control is solid
- Fewer reps (8 per side) in the first 1–2 weeks of Month 2

## Harder (progression)
- Single-leg on a block for full heel drop (standard from Month 2)
- Hold a dumbbell or plate on the thigh of the working leg (Month 4–5: 8–10 kg range when programmed)
- Slow the eccentric to 4–5 seconds
- Pause 2 seconds at the bottom stretch before rising
- Slight pause at the top with maximal squeeze
