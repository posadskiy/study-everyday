# Bulgarian Split Squat

Used in: **Session A (Tuesday)** and **Session B (Thursday)**. See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for full program.

**What it trains:** eccentric quads, glutes, single-leg balance — the closest gym exercise to downhill running stress.

## How to do it
1. Stand ~60–70 cm in front of a bench or chair
2. Place the top of one foot on the bench behind you (laces down)
3. Front foot: far enough forward that your knee does not travel past your toes at the bottom
4. Lower your body straight down over **3–4 seconds** — back knee drops toward the floor
5. Stop when front thigh is roughly parallel to the floor (or as low as comfortable)
6. Drive up through the heel of the front foot

**Key detail:** most of the weight should be on the front leg. If it feels like your back leg is doing the work, step the front foot further forward.

## Common mistakes
- Front knee caving inward — squeeze glute and push knee out over the little toe
- Leaning heavily forward — slight forward lean is fine, but chest should stay relatively tall
- Back foot on toes instead of laces — this stresses the ankle unnecessarily
- Going too fast — the eccentric (lowering) is the point

## Easier (beginner)
- Hold a wall or TRX strap for balance
- Use a lower surface (folded mat) under the back foot instead of a full bench
- Reduce range of motion — just lower a few centimetres until you build stability

## Harder (progression)
- Hold dumbbells or add a barbell
- Increase eccentric to 4–5 seconds
- Add a pause at the bottom (2 seconds)
- Front foot elevated too (deficit split squat) for extra range
