# Single-Leg Squat (Pistol Progression)

Used in: **Session A (Tuesday)** and **Session B (Thursday)**. See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for full program.

**What it trains:** glute and quad integration, single-leg balance and stability.

## How to do it (Phase 1 — box version)
1. Stand on one leg in front of a box or chair (40–50 cm height)
2. Extend arms forward for counterbalance
3. Slowly lower yourself to sit on the box — control the descent over 3–4 seconds
4. Stand back up driving through the heel
5. Do not bounce or flop — touch the box, pause, then stand

## How to do it (Phase 2 — full range)
1. Stand on one leg, arms extended or crossed on chest
2. Lower as deep as control allows (aim for thigh parallel to floor or lower)
3. Drive up through heel

## Common mistakes
- Knee caving inward — the most common error; push the knee out to track over the little toe
- Leaning heavily to the side — some lean is normal; exaggerated lean means hip is weak
- Using momentum to bounce at the bottom — remove the box when you can stand up smoothly, not before

## Easier (beginner)
- Use a higher box (50–60 cm) to reduce range
- Hold a door frame or TRX for balance assistance
- Squat onto two stacked cushions and reduce height over weeks

## Harder (progression)
- Full pistol squat (leg extended forward, full depth)
- Add dumbbells
- Elevated front foot (deficit pistol)
