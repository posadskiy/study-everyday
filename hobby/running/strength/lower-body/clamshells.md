# Clamshells

Used in: **Session A (Tuesday)** and **Session B (Thursday)**. See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for full program.

**What it trains:** gluteus medius — lateral hip stabilizer. Weakness here is the primary cause of IT band syndrome and knee pain in runners.

## How to do it
1. Lie on your side, hips stacked, knees bent to about 45 degrees
2. Keep feet together throughout the movement
3. Rotate the top knee upward (like a clamshell opening) as high as possible without letting the hip roll backward
4. Lower slowly and repeat

**Key detail:** the hip must not rock backward. If your pelvis tilts, you are using your lower back instead of your gluteus medius — reduce range.

## Common mistakes
- Rolling the pelvis backward to gain extra range — reduces the exercise to a hip rotation instead of glute activation
- Moving too fast — slow, controlled reps are more effective
- Bending the top leg more to make it "easier" — this changes the mechanics

## Easier (beginner)
- No band, bodyweight only
- Smaller range of motion — just a few centimetres, keeping pelvis locked

## Harder (progression)
- Add a resistance band around the knees
- Move to a stronger band
- Perform standing with a band (fire hydrant pattern)
