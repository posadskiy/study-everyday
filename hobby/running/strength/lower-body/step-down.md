# Step-Down (Box or Stair)

Used in: **Session A (Tuesday)** and **Session B (Thursday)**. See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for full program.

**What it trains:** eccentric quad strength — your shock absorbers on downhill terrain.

## How to do it
1. Stand on a box or stair step, 30–40 cm high, one foot at the edge
2. Extend the other leg forward slightly (off the step)
3. Slowly bend the standing knee and lower the free foot toward the floor over **3–4 seconds**
4. Touch the floor lightly with your heel — do not transfer weight
5. Push back up through the standing heel to starting position
6. Complete all reps on one side, then switch

**Key detail:** the slow lowering (eccentric phase) is the training stimulus. The return is just reset.

## Common mistakes
- Dropping fast — gravity doing the work defeats the purpose
- Knee caving inward as you lower — if this happens, go shallower until you build control
- Gripping with the foot (curling toes) — keep foot relaxed, drive through heel
- Leaning forward at the torso — stay upright, chest tall

## Easier (beginner)
- Use a lower step (15–20 cm)
- Hold a wall or door frame for balance
- Lower only to a partial range (half bend) until knee stability improves

## Harder (progression)
- Increase box height (40–50 cm)
- Add a weight vest or hold dumbbells
- Slow the eccentric to 5–6 seconds
- Add a pause at the bottom for 2 seconds before returning
