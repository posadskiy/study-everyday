# Bird Dog

Used in: **Session A (Tuesday)** and **Session B (Thursday)**. See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for full program.

**What it trains:** anti-rotation core stability, glute and shoulder coordination, spinal stability under movement. Directly trains the coordination pattern used in running (opposite arm/leg).

## How to do it
1. Start on all fours — hands under shoulders, knees under hips
2. Keep your back flat (neutral spine — not arched, not rounded)
3. Simultaneously extend your right arm forward (thumb up) and left leg back
4. Both extended limbs reach parallel to the floor — hold 2–3 seconds
5. Return slowly and switch sides
6. Hips must stay level throughout — no rotation

**Key detail:** imagine balancing a glass of water on your lower back. If it would spill, your hips are rotating.

## Common mistakes
- Hips rotating or tilting — the most common error; slow down and reduce range until control is there
- Arching the lower back as the leg extends — brace the core first, then extend
- Raising the arm and leg too high — parallel to the floor is enough
- Going too fast — slow reps (2 seconds out, 2 seconds hold, 2 seconds back) beat fast reps

## Easier (beginner)
- Only move the leg (keep hand on floor)
- Only move the arm (keep knee on floor)
- Reduce the hold at extension to 1 second

## Harder (progression)
- Add a resistance band around the wrists or ankles
- Perform from a push-up position (high plank bird dog) — significantly harder for core stability
- Pause 3–5 seconds at full extension
- Draw a square in the air with the extended hand or foot before returning
