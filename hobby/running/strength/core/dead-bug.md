# Dead Bug

**What it trains:** deep core stabilizers (transverse abdominis), anti-extension stability. More effective for running than crunches or sit-ups.

## How to do it
1. Lie on your back, arms pointing straight up toward the ceiling
2. Bring knees up to 90 degrees (shins parallel to floor, knees above hips)
3. **Press your lower back flat into the floor** — this must stay throughout the movement
4. Slowly lower your right arm back toward the floor above your head while simultaneously extending your left leg toward the floor
5. Lower both to just above the floor (do not touch), pause 1 second
6. Return to start and switch sides (left arm + right leg)
7. That is 1 rep per side

**The most important detail:** lower back must not arch off the floor at any point. If it does, reduce your range of motion.

## Common mistakes
- Lower back lifting off the floor — the most common error; reduce range, slow down
- Holding breath — exhale as you extend the arm and leg, inhale on the return
- Moving too fast — this is a slow, controlled exercise
- Not fully extending the leg — a slight bend is fine for beginners, but aim for a nearly straight leg eventually

## Easier (beginner)
- Only move the arms (keep both legs at 90 degrees)
- Only move the legs (keep both arms vertical)
- Keep the moving leg bent at 90 degrees rather than extending it straight

## Harder (progression)
- Add a pause at full extension (2–3 seconds)
- Hold a light dumbbell in each hand
- Straighten the leg fully and hover it 10 cm above the floor
- Add a resistance band around the feet
