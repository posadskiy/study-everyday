# Thoracic Spine Rotation

Part of the [daily mobility routine](README.md). See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for program context.

**What it trains:** upper back (thoracic) rotation — needed for arm swing and balance on technical terrain. Stiff thoracic spine leads to overcompensation in the lower back and neck.

## How to do it (seated or on all fours)

**Seated:** sit upright in a chair or on the floor. Cross your arms over your chest. Rotate your upper body as far as possible to one side, hold 1–2 seconds, return. Repeat 10 times per side, 2 rounds.

**On all fours (thread the needle):**
1. Start on hands and knees
2. Slide one hand along the floor under your body, reaching to the opposite side
3. Follow the hand with your eyes and upper back — your shoulder drops toward the floor
4. Hold 2–3 seconds at maximum rotation
5. Return and repeat 8–10 times per side

## Common mistakes
- Rotating from the lower back instead of the upper back — try to keep the pelvis still (all fours version helps with this)
- Forcing the range — this should be a smooth, controlled movement
- Going too fast — slow rotations with breath get more range than rapid swings

## Easier
- Seated version in a chair (less demand on hips and lower back)
- Smaller range of motion

## Harder
- Add a light resistance band anchored at shoulder height — rotate away from the anchor
- Combine with a squat hold (thoracic rotation in a deep squat position)
