# Hip Flexor Stretch

Part of the [daily mobility routine](README.md). See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for program context.

**What it trains:** lengthens the hip flexors (psoas, iliacus, rectus femoris). Trail runners keep hips in flexion for hours — this directly counteracts that.

## How to do it
1. Kneel on one knee — back knee on the floor, front foot flat on the floor
2. Keep your torso upright (do not lean forward)
3. Gently drive your hips forward until you feel a stretch in the front of the back leg's hip and upper thigh
4. Hold 60–90 seconds per side, breathing slowly
5. Do not force it — the stretch should feel like moderate tension, not pain

**Deeper variation:** from the same position, raise the back knee's arm overhead and side-bend slightly away from the back leg — opens the hip flexor along a different vector.

## Common mistakes
- Leaning forward at the torso — this removes the stretch and loads the lower back instead
- Holding breath — breathe fully, the stretch deepens slightly on each exhale
- Rushing — 60 seconds minimum per side; less than this does not change tissue

## Easier
- Place a folded mat under the back knee if the floor is uncomfortable
- Reduce range — just a slight hip shift forward, build over weeks

## Harder
- Add a slight posterior pelvic tilt (tuck the tailbone under) before pushing hips forward — intensifies the stretch
- Perform a couch stretch: back foot elevated on a wall or couch, back knee on the floor
