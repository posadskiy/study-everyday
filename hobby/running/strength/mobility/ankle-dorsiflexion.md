# Ankle Dorsiflexion

Part of the [daily mobility routine](README.md). See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for program context.

**What it trains:** ankle mobility — the range of motion that allows the knee to travel forward over the foot during a squat or uphill step. Poor ankle dorsiflexion causes compensations (heel rise, knee valgus) all the way up the chain.

## How to do it
1. Kneel in front of a wall, foot 10–15 cm away from the wall
2. Drive your knee forward toward the wall while keeping your heel flat on the floor
3. If your knee touches the wall easily, move the foot farther back until it just barely reaches
4. Hold 1–2 seconds at end range, return, repeat
5. 2–3 minutes total per foot (not a static hold — rhythmic repetitions work better here)

**Self-test:** if your foot needs to be less than 10 cm from the wall for the knee to touch, dorsiflexion is significantly limited.

## Common mistakes
- Heel lifting off the floor — this defeats the purpose; keep heel down even if range reduces
- Knee collapsing inward — push it out to track over the second toe
- Treating it as a one-time stretch — this needs daily repetition to create lasting change

## Easier
- Start closer to the wall, work farther away over weeks
- Seated ankle circles to warm the joint first

## Harder
- Add load: lean into the wall with hands (more weight through the ankle)
- Banded distraction: loop a resistance band around the ankle joint from behind (pulls the joint back, creating more space)
