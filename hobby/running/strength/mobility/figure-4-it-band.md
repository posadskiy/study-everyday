# IT Band and Lateral Hip — Figure-4 Stretch

Part of the [daily mobility routine](README.md). See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for program context.

**What it trains:** piriformis, lateral hip rotators, IT band complex. Tightness here causes IT band syndrome — the most common trail running injury.

## How to do it
1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on floor
2. Cross your right ankle over your left knee (like the number 4)
3. Flex the right foot (pull toes toward shin) — this protects the knee
4. Draw both legs toward your chest by lifting the left foot off the floor
5. Reach through the gap and hold behind the left thigh (or shin)
6. Hold 60 seconds per side

**Key detail:** if you feel no stretch, draw the legs closer to your chest.

## Common mistakes
- Foot not flexed — risk of knee strain; always dorsiflex the crossed foot
- Not drawing legs close enough — the further you pull, the more the lateral hip opens
- Holding too short — 60 seconds minimum per side

## Easier
- Keep the bottom foot on the floor (less range, less intensity)
- Place a strap around the bottom thigh to pull without shoulder tension

## Harder
- Perform seated (pigeon pose variation): sit on the floor, one shin in front of you parallel to the body, lean forward over it
- Add a forward lean over the crossed leg while seated on a chair
