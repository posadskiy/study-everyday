# Calf Stretch

Part of the [daily mobility routine](README.md). See `../../technique/06-strength-and-mobility.md` for program context.

**What it trains:** lengthens both heads of the calf — gastrocnemius (with the knee straight) and soleus (with the knee bent). Running shortens the calves; without this, the Achilles takes more strain and ankle dorsiflexion stays limited.

## How to do it
**Straight-knee version (gastrocnemius):**
1. Stand facing a wall, hands flat on the wall at chest height
2. Step one foot back ~80 cm, keep that back leg straight, heel pressed into the floor
3. Lean into the wall by bending the front knee — back leg stays straight, back heel stays down
4. Feel the stretch in the upper calf (belly of the muscle)
5. Hold 30 seconds per side

**Bent-knee version (soleus):**
1. Same setup, but bring the back foot in closer (~50 cm)
2. Bend the back knee slightly, keep the heel down
3. Feel the stretch lower in the calf, just above the Achilles
4. Hold 30 seconds per side

Do both versions on each side — straight knee first, then bent knee. Total ~2 min for both calves.

## Common mistakes
- Heel lifting — kills the stretch; keep both heels flat on the floor
- Foot turned outward — keep toes pointing straight at the wall
- Bouncing — hold the stretch still, don't pulse

## Easier
- Reduce distance from wall (smaller stretch)
- Use a step: heel hanging off the back of a step, gently let the heel drop

## Harder
- Step further from the wall
- Add a 5–10 sec contract-relax: push the ball of the foot into the floor for 5 sec, then relax deeper into the stretch
