# Lesson 00 — What Is Mountain Running?

> Before training, understand what world you are entering and where you fit in it.

---

## The Discipline Landscape

Mountain running is not one sport. It is an umbrella of related disciplines. Knowing the differences helps you choose races, set goals, and understand what training each requires.

### Trail Running
Running on unpaved paths, forest tracks, and dirt roads. Elevation gain is modest to significant. This is the broadest category and includes the majority of local events.

- Distance: 10 km to 50 km typical
- Elevation: 200–2000 m D+ typical
- Terrain: mostly runnable, some technical sections
- **Where you are now:** your 32 km / 1750 m D+ race was a solid trail run

### Mountain Running
Emphasis on significant vertical gain and alpine terrain. More technical underfoot. Weather exposure. Often above treeline.

- Distance: 20–50 km typical
- Elevation: 1500–4000 m D+ typical
- Terrain: rocky, scree, exposed ridges
- **Where you are heading in years 1–3**

### Ultra Trail
Any distance beyond marathon (42.2 km) on trail. Ultra does not mean harder per km — most ultras are run at lower intensity than shorter races. The challenge is duration and logistics.

- Distance: 50 km to 170+ km (UTMB)
- Elevation: 3000–10000 m D+
- Time on feet: 6 hours to multi-day
- **Possible in years 3–5 once you have the base**

### Skyrunning
Technical high-altitude racing. Often involves hands-on-rock scrambling, very steep terrain, exposed ridges. Requires true alpine skills.

- Defined by: altitude > 2000 m, average slope > 30%, technical terrain
- Examples: Dolomiti Skyrace, Limone Extreme
- **Something to watch for now, consider later**

### Vertical Kilometer (VK)
Pure uphill race covering 1000 m of elevation gain in approximately 1 km horizontal distance. Brutal intensity. 30–60 minutes for elites.

- Great for building uphill power
- Short enough to enter while still a beginner
- Often held alongside longer mountain races

### Cross-Country (XC) Trail
Shorter, fast, less technical. Think park runs on dirt. Useful for speed training.

## Where You Fit Right Now

```
Beginner Trail → Intermediate Trail → Mountain Running → Ultra Trail
     ↑
  You are here
```

What you are building now:

- **Technical skills** (downhill technique, terrain reading)
- **Running economy** (how efficiently you move at aerobic pace)
- **Aerobic base** (the engine everything else runs on)
- **Strength** (the chassis that prevents injury)

---

## How Mountain Running Differs from Hiking

You come from hiking, which is an advantage. Your connective tissue (tendons, ligaments) is adapted to mountain terrain. The differences to internalize:

| | Hiking | Trail Running |
|---|---|---|
| Effort intensity | Low, sustained | Variable, often moderate |
| Downhill technique | Any gait fine | Specific technique required to avoid injury |
| Fueling | Can eat real food, stops | Must fuel while moving |
| Time pressure | None | Race cutoffs, daylight windows |
| Footwear | Stiff, supportive | Flexible, low-drop |
| Poles | Common | Strategic (not always) |

The biggest mental shift: **running is not fast hiking.** It uses different muscles, different energy systems, and requires different technique. Your hiking endurance transfers; your hiking mechanics do not.
