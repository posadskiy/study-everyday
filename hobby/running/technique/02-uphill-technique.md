# Lesson 02 — Uphill Technique

> Uphills feel hard. They are supposed to. The goal is not to make them easy — it is to not waste energy here that you need for later.

---

## The Fundamental Principle: Economy Over Pride

The single most important lesson about uphills in mountain running: **power hiking a steep section is faster and more economical than running it badly.**

Many beginners feel they "should be running" every section. Elites in 100-mile races walk uphills they could technically run because the energy cost is not worth it. Your ego is not a training tool.

**The rule of thumb:** if your heart rate is above Zone 3 and you are not going faster than power hiking would take you, hike.

---

## When to Run vs. When to Power Hike

### Run the uphill when:
- Gradient is below ~10–12%
- You can maintain conversation while running it
- Heart rate stays below threshold (roughly 75–80% of max)
- The section is short enough that the HR spike is brief

### Power hike when:
- Gradient is above 12–15%
- Running it would send you into the red zone
- It follows a long climb (fatigue already accumulated)
- You are in the middle or late stages of a race

### How to calculate gradient
- Elevation gain ÷ distance × 100
- 100m gain over 1000m horizontal = 10% gradient
- Most trails with sustained running: 8–15%
- Steep Karst and alpine trails: 25–40%

## Running Uphill — Mechanics

When gradient is runnable:

### Body Position
- Lean forward slightly from the ankles (same principle as flat running, but more pronounced)
- Chest open, do not collapse forward over the knees
- Eyes on the trail ahead, not watching your feet
- Avoid hunching — it restricts breathing

### Stride
- **Shorten your stride significantly** compared to flat running
- Push off the ball of the foot, drive from the glutes and hip flexors
- Higher cadence compensates for shorter stride: think "quick little steps"
- Do not try to power through with long lunging strides — this destroys quads

### Arms
- Drive arms actively on uphills — they contribute real momentum
- Pump elbows back strongly, not just swinging loosely
- Short powerful arm swings, synchronized with steps
- Arms become more important on steep terrain

### Breathing
- Expect breathing rate to increase sharply on uphills
- This is normal and unavoidable
- Use it as a pacing signal: if you cannot speak a short sentence, you are going too hard
- See Lesson 04 for full breathing detail

## Power Hiking — A Skill, Not a Failure

Power hiking is a technical skill. Done right, it is nearly as fast as running on steep terrain and costs a fraction of the energy.

### Technique
- **Hands on knees:** on very steep sections, place both hands just above your knees and push. This transfers work from legs to arms and upper body. This is a legal and efficient technique used by elites.
- **Full foot contact:** plant the whole foot on the ground (not just forefoot), use the entire leg including hamstrings and glutes
- **Upright posture:** avoid excessive forward lean — it compresses hip flexors and restricts breathing
- **Consistent pace:** find a rhythm you can maintain for 20–30 min without stopping

### With Poles
Poles multiply power hiking efficiency enormously. On sustained climbs above 15%, poles are worth using. They:
- Off-load leg work to arms and upper body
- Improve balance on loose terrain
- Reduce RPE (perceived effort) by 5–8% on uphills
- Allow arms to contribute momentum

**Basic pole technique for uphills:**
1. Plant pole beside or slightly behind your foot
2. Push down and back as you step forward
3. Both poles alternate with opposite foot (left pole, right foot)
4. On very steep terrain, both poles can plant simultaneously with each step

See the gear section for pole recommendations.

## Saving Your Quads for the Descent

This is strategic, not just a technique point.

Quads do the braking work on downhills. Once they fatigue, your downhill speed collapses and injury risk rises. Every unnecessary effort on uphills costs you descent performance.

**Concrete decisions:**
- On a mixed climb/descent course, hold back 10–15% on the uphills
- Walk the last 20% of a climb before a long descent to arrive at the top with fresher legs
- On out-and-back courses: be conservative on the way up, stronger on the way down

## Hill Repeats — Training for Uphills

**The most effective single workout for mountain running fitness.**

### Basic Hill Repeat Protocol (start here)
- Find a hill with 100–200m elevation gain (8–15% gradient)
- Run up at strong effort (Z3–Z4, breathing hard but sustainable)
- Walk or easy jog back down (recovery)
- Repeat 4–6 times
- Frequency: once per week

**Duration:** 3–5 min per repeat is ideal for aerobic development

### Progression
- Month 1–2: 4 repeats × 3 min climb
- Month 3–4: 5 repeats × 4 min climb
- Month 5–6: 6 repeats × 5 min climb
- Once solid: add longer continuous climbs (30–60 min sustained)

**Karst local options:**
- Nanos plateau road (from Razdrto) — long sustained gradient
- Trails from Lipica area
- Any of the paths rising from Sežana toward Kras plateau edge

---

## Vertical Kilometer Races as Training

Consider entering a Vertical Kilometer event in your first year. These are uphill-only races (1000m elevation, ~1km horizontal). Benefits:
- Forces you to develop uphill running efficiency
- Relatively short (45–90 min for a beginner)
- Excellent benchmark for progress
- Available in Slovenia and northern Italy

## Key Takeaways

1. Power hiking is a skill, not a concession. Learn it.
2. Shorten your stride, pump your arms, lean from ankles.
3. Use poles on sustained steep climbs (15%+).
4. Protect your quads — you need them for the descent.
5. Heart rate and breathing rate are better pacing signals than GPS pace on uphills.
