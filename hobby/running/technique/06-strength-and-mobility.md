# Lesson 06 — Strength and Mobility

> Running alone will not keep you healthy for 20 years. Strength training is the structural maintenance that makes long-term running possible.

---

## Why Mountain Runners Must Strength Train

Trail running is a high-impact, high-variety activity. Every rocky landing, every ankle correction on loose terrain, every steep descent is a demand on muscle, tendon, and joint beyond what flat road running produces.

Without supporting strength work:
- Tendons and joints accumulate load faster than they can adapt
- Imbalances develop (typically quad-dominant, weak glutes, tight hips)
- Injuries emerge as volume increases

With consistent strength work (2–3 sessions/week, 30–40 min each):
- Running economy improves 3–8% (you use less energy at the same pace)
- Injury rate drops significantly
- Downhill performance improves dramatically
- You can sustain higher training volumes

## The Foundation: What to Train

### Priority 1: Eccentric Quad Strength (Downhill Protection)

As explained in Lesson 03, your quads are your shock absorbers on descent. Eccentric strength is the capacity to generate force while the muscle lengthens.

**Key exercises:**

#### Step-Down (Box or Stair)
- Stand on a box or step (30–40 cm high)
- Slowly step one foot down to the floor level over 3–4 seconds
- Control the movement entirely — do not let gravity drop you
- Touch the floor lightly and return to standing
- 3 sets × 10 reps per leg
- Progress: increase box height, add weight vest

#### Bulgarian Split Squat
- Rear foot elevated on bench, front foot forward
- Lower slowly (3–4 seconds) to 90 degrees at knee
- Drive up through the heel of front foot
- 3 sets × 8–10 reps per leg
- This is the closest gym approximation of downhill running stress

#### Wall Sit with Eccentric
- Hold a wall sit at 90 degrees for 30–45 seconds
- Alternate with slow sit-to-stand at single leg

---

### Priority 2: Glute and Hip Strength (Power and Stability)

Weak glutes are the most common contributing factor to knee pain, IT band syndrome, and running inefficiency in trail runners.

#### Single-Leg Squat (Pistol Progression)
- Phase 1: squat onto a box or chair, control the lowering
- Phase 2: full single-leg squat as range allows
- 3 sets × 8–10 reps per leg

#### Hip Thrust / Glute Bridge
- Shoulders on bench, feet on floor, drive hips up through heels
- Hold 1 second at top, squeeze glutes hard
- Progress: barbell loaded, single-leg
- 3 sets × 12–15 reps

#### Clamshells
- Lying on side, knees bent at 90 degrees
- Lift top knee while keeping feet together
- Targets gluteus medius — critical for lateral knee stability
- 3 sets × 15–20 reps per side

#### Lateral Band Walks
- Resistance band around ankles
- Walk sideways maintaining slight squat
- 3 sets × 15 steps each direction

---

### Priority 3: Calf and Ankle Strength (Tendon Protection)

Achilles tendinopathy is a common overuse injury in trail runners. Strong calves and loaded tendons are protective.

#### Calf Raises (Loaded)
- Stand on edge of step, heel below step level
- Lower heel fully (3–4 seconds), raise onto toes
- Both feet first, progress to single-leg
- Add weight when bodyweight becomes easy
- 3 sets × 15 reps (build to single-leg)

#### Tibialis Anterior Raise
- Stand against wall, heels 30cm from wall
- Lift toes toward shins, lower slowly
- Prevents shin splints and supports ankle
- 3 sets × 20 reps

#### Single-Leg Balance with Movement
- Stand on one foot for 30–60 seconds
- Progress: close eyes, stand on foam pad, catch and throw ball
- This trains the proprioceptive system — your ankle's automatic stabilization

---

### Priority 4: Core Stability (Not Core Strength)

Mountain runners need a stable core that resists rotation and flexion under load — not a six-pack. The goal is efficient force transfer between legs and upper body.

#### Dead Bug
- Lie on back, arms vertical, knees at 90 degrees in the air
- Slowly lower opposite arm and leg toward floor while keeping lower back pressed to floor
- Return and switch sides
- 3 sets × 10 reps per side
- This is more effective than crunches or sit-ups for running

#### Plank Variations
- Standard plank: 3 × 30–45 seconds
- Side plank: 3 × 20–30 seconds per side (targets lateral hip stability)
- Progress: add leg lift, use unstable surface

#### Bird Dog
- On all fours, extend opposite arm and leg
- Maintain level hips throughout
- 3 sets × 10 per side

---

### Priority 5: Mobility Work

Mobility training maintains the range of motion required for efficient running mechanics and reduces injury risk from repetitive movement patterns.

**Daily (10 min, can be morning or evening):**

#### Hip Flexor Stretch (Priority 1)
- Kneeling lunge position, back knee on ground
- Drive hips forward until you feel a pull in the front of the back leg's hip
- Hold 60–90 seconds per side
- Trail runners spend hours in hip flexion — this counteracts that

#### Ankle Dorsiflexion
- Kneel in front of a wall, foot 10–15 cm away
- Drive knee toward wall while keeping heel down
- If knee reaches wall easily, move foot farther
- 2–3 min total, both feet
- Poor ankle dorsiflexion causes compensations up the chain

#### Hamstring Stretch
- Seated, single leg extended, gentle forward fold
- Or: lying on back with leg raised (more controlled)
- Hold 60 seconds per side

#### IT Band and Hip Rotation (Figure-4 Stretch)
- Lie on back, cross one ankle over opposite knee
- Draw knees toward chest
- Targets the lateral hip and IT band indirectly
- Hold 60 seconds per side

#### Thoracic Spine Rotation
- Seated or on all fours, rotate upper body open toward ceiling
- Mountain running requires constant spinal rotation for arm swing and balance
- 10 rotations per side, 2 rounds

## The Weekly Strength Schedule

> Detailed exercise descriptions, common mistakes, and progressions: see [`strength/`](../strength/)
>
> **Phase-specific volume** (sets, reps, load) is in [`../progression/`](../progression/) for the first 6 months. The tables below are the **mature program** — used from month 6 onward. Earlier phases use scaled-down versions.

**2 sessions/week minimum. Default schedule: Tuesday and Thursday, 7:30am — before breakfast, several hours before the afternoon run.**

| Day | Session | When |
|-----|---------|------|
| Tuesday | **Session A** — lower body + core | 7:30am |
| Thursday | **Session B** — glute + core | 7:30am |

Morning strength + afternoon run is the optimal split — full recovery between the two sessions. Have a small snack before if needed (banana, yogurt). Eat full breakfast after the session.

### Session A — Tuesday (35–40 min) — Lower Body + Core

Full mature prescription. For month 1–5 volumes see [`../progression/`](../progression/).

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Calf raises (straight knee) | 3 × 12 | Single-leg, weighted; slow eccentric (4 sec down) |
| Calf raises (bent knee, seated) | 3 × 10 | Targets soleus specifically |
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 8 per leg | Weighted; slow eccentric (4 sec) |
| Step-downs | 3 × 10 per leg | 30–40 cm step; weighted vest if available |
| Dead bug | 3 × 12 per side | Lower back flat to floor throughout |
| Mobility | 10 min | Hip flexors + ankle dorsiflexion |

### Session B — Thursday (35–40 min) — Glute + Core

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Hip thrust | 3 × 12 | Weighted; progress to single-leg |
| Single-leg squat | 3 × 8 per leg | Box version first; build to full range |
| Lateral band walks | 3 × 15 each direction | Quarter-squat position throughout |
| Bird dog | 3 × 10 per side | Hips level; slow and controlled |
| Side plank with hip dip | 3 × 10 per side | Static side plank (30–45 sec) is the regression |
| Tibialis raises | 3 × 20 | Against wall, heels 30 cm from wall |
| Mobility | 10 min | IT band (figure-4) + hamstrings + thoracic |

## Progression Principles

For the first 6 months, follow the phase-specific progression in [`../progression/`](../progression/) — bodyweight in month 1, light resistance in months 2–3, loaded versions in months 4–6.

**Year 2+:** Maintain 2×/week throughout the year. During peak race training (4–6 weeks before key races), reduce intensity/volume slightly to avoid excess fatigue.

## Key Takeaways

1. 2–3 strength sessions per week for the first year. This is non-negotiable.
2. Eccentric exercises (step-downs, slow split squats) are more valuable than standard gym work.
3. Glute weakness causes more trail running injuries than anything else. Train them.
4. Mobility work daily, even if 10 minutes. Hip flexors and ankles first.
5. Strength training improves running performance directly — it is not separate from running, it is part of it.
