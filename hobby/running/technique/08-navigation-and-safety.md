# Lesson 08 — Navigation and Safety

> The mountain does not know you are a trail runner. Respect it accordingly.

---

## The Mindset Shift from Hiking to Trail Running

As a hiker, you likely moved slowly enough to stop, check a map, look around, and make decisions with time. Trail running changes this:
- You cover ground faster, giving less time to recognize a wrong turn
- You carry less — lighter pack means less emergency gear
- You are often alone, and sometimes in poor conditions
- Fatigue late in a run impairs decision-making

The solution is not to slow down trail running to hiking pace. It is to develop **proactive navigation skills** so that you rarely need emergency decisions.

## Digital Navigation

### Komoot
The best all-purpose trail running navigation app for the Slovenia/Italy region.

- **Route planning:** search and preview routes before leaving home
- **Turn-by-turn on phone or watch:** works offline once downloaded
- **Community routes:** local runners have mapped trails in Karst, Nanos, Triglav area
- **Elevation profiles:** always check D+ and D- before committing to a route
- **Download:** always download offline maps before heading out

**Workflow:**
1. Plan route on Komoot desktop or app the day before
2. Download to phone and sync to GPS watch if supported
3. Verify route makes sense (check elevation profile, distance, terrain type)
4. Leave route details with someone who knows where you are going

### Wikiloc
Community-created trail database. Good for finding known trails in Slovenia and Friuli. Less polished than Komoot but more local routes in some areas.

### OSMand / Maps.me
OpenStreetMap-based apps. Excellent offline maps, free. More raw than Komoot but comprehensive. Good backup navigation app.

### GPS Watch with Maps
Once you have a GPS watch (Garmin, Coros), many models support breadcrumb navigation or full maps. Loading your Komoot route to the watch is the standard workflow. You can keep phone in vest for emergency backup.

## Reading Terrain and Staying on Route

### Before the Run
- Study the elevation profile: know where the major climbs and descents are
- Identify navigation checkpoints: peaks, col crossings, stream crossings, road crossings
- Note time estimates for each section — if you are significantly behind, something is wrong

### During the Run
- **Mark the junction:** whenever you take a turn at a fork, note it consciously. Do not blindly follow the blue line.
- **Trail markers:** Slovenia uses colored waymarkers on rocks and trees. Italy uses CAI markers (red/white stripes with route numbers). Learn to recognize these.
- **At any doubt: stop, consult map, confirm.** Do not run on when unsure. Three minutes of map-reading saves three kilometers of wrong trail.
- **Backtrack confidently:** if you are wrong, return to the last certain point. Running down a wrong ridge is always slower than going back.

### Loss of Signal / App Failure
GPS apps can fail. Common causes: battery drain, phone overheating (summer sun in vest), wet touchscreen.

**Backup plan:**
- Always have offline maps loaded, not just live satellite
- Know the direction of the nearest road from your route (generally)
- In Slovenia/Italy: most mountain paths lead eventually to a marked trail or road
- Screenshot the route before leaving so you have a static image if app fails

## Weather and the Karst/Alpine Context

### The Bora (Burja in Slovenian)
The Bora is a severe cold, dry, gusty wind from the northeast that funnels through the Karst region and Vipava Valley. It can reach 100–150+ km/h in gusts.

**What you need to know:**
- Bora can appear quickly and is genuinely dangerous on exposed ridges
- It causes rapid body cooling through convection (wind chill)
- Standing on exposed Karst rock in strong Bora = serious hypothermia risk
- Always check the Arso.si (Slovenian weather service) forecast before runs above the valley
- If Bora is forecast at strength 7+ (Beaufort), avoid exposed ridges entirely

### Alpine Thunderstorms
In the Julian Alps, Dolomites, and higher Slovenian ranges:
- Afternoon thunderstorms are common June–August
- Plan to be off exposed ridges by 12–1pm on summer days
- If you hear thunder: descend immediately. Do not wait to see lightning.
- Lightning on exposed rock or ridge is a genuine danger

### Fog on Karst
Fog can set in quickly in the Karst, particularly in autumn and early spring.
- Navigation becomes very difficult without GPS
- The terrain looks the same in every direction (flat limestone plateau)
- Sinkholes are a real hazard — you can walk directly into one if you leave the path
- If caught in Karst fog: stop, use your GPS, do not leave the marked path to shortcut

### Temperature Drops
Above 1500m in the Alps, temperature drops 6–7°C per 1000m of elevation gain. Starting in Sežana at 20°C, you may face 8–10°C at Triglav elevation even in summer. Always carry a wind layer.

## Solo Running Safety

### The Non-Negotiable Rules

1. **Tell someone your plan.** Route, start time, expected finish time, what to do if you do not return by a specific hour. This is not excessive — it is standard mountain protocol.

2. **Carry a fully charged phone.** Your phone is your emergency beacon. Put it in your vest, not your pocket where it can fall.

3. **Know the emergency numbers.**
   - Slovenia mountain rescue: **112** (universal emergency) — connects to GRZS (Gorska Reševalna Služba)
   - Italy mountain rescue: **118** (general medical) or **117** (police who coordinate rescue)
   - GRZS also has a free mobile app: **112.si** which sends your GPS coordinates to rescue services

4. **Do not run beyond your skills in bad conditions.** Being comfortable in good conditions does not mean being safe in bad conditions. The same trail is a different challenge in rain, snow, fog, or darkness.

### Minimum Kit for Solo Runs (2+ hours)

| Item | Why |
|------|-----|
| Phone with GPS (charged) | Navigation, emergency |
| Waterproof wind/rain layer | Temperature management |
| Emergency space blanket | Weighs 50g, saves lives |
| Headlamp | If run extends into dusk |
| Water (500–1500ml depending on distance) | Dehydration impairs judgment |
| Food (2–3 gels or equivalent) | Blood sugar, emergency energy |
| Basic first aid: blister kit, bandage | Mechanical failures |
| Identification / cash | In case of rescue need |

This kit fits in a running vest without meaningfully increasing weight.

## First Aid Basics for Trail Runners

### Ankle Sprains (Most Common Trail Injury)
- RICE immediately: Rest, Ice (cold stream water), Compression, Elevation
- Grade 1 (mild): walk out, run in 5–7 days
- Grade 2 (moderate): pain/swelling, walk out carefully
- Grade 3 (severe): cannot weight-bear, call rescue
- Prevention: ankle strength training (see Lesson 06), look ahead when running (Lesson 01)

### Hypoglycemia (Bonking)
- Signs: sudden weakness, confusion, inability to think clearly, shaking
- Treatment: stop, eat immediately — gel, sugar, anything available
- Recovery: 10–15 minutes
- Prevention: proactive fueling (see Lesson 07)

### Hypothermia
- Signs: shivering, poor coordination, confusion
- Early treatment: insulating layer, space blanket, movement to generate heat, food and water
- Serious case: stop moving (movement accelerates heat loss in some stages), call rescue, protect from wind

### Dehydration
- Signs: headache, dark urine (before you left), dizziness, cramping
- Treatment: small frequent sips, add electrolytes, reduce pace
- Prevention: see Lesson 07

## Karst-Specific Route Recommendations for Training

**Starting from Sežana:**
- **Nanos plateau** (via Razdrto): sustained climb, great for uphill training, spectacular Karst views. 2hr drive or 1.5hr via trail.
- **Slavnik** (1028m): closest significant summit from Sežana. Accessible loop. Good for consistent hill repeats.
- **Stena trail / Lipica-Tomaj area:** local Karst trails, moderate technical terrain, good for weekly runs.
- **Kras cross-country loops:** flat to rolling limestone plateau. Good for easy long runs.

**Nearby in Italy (within 1 hour):**
- **Carso/Collio trails**: immediately over border, well-marked, similar terrain
- **Canin massif trails** (Friuli): more technical, higher elevation, 1.5h away

## Key Takeaways

1. Always tell someone your route and expected return time.
2. Download offline maps before leaving. Do not rely on cellular data in mountains.
3. Know the Bora — check Arso.si before runs on exposed Karst.
4. Carry a space blanket, phone, and basic food on any run over 2 hours.
5. In doubt about route: stop and check. Do not run on uncertain.
