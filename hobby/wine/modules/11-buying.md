# Module 11 — Buying Wine

Knowing how to buy wine is where theory meets practice. This module covers every buying channel — wine shops, restaurants, online, wine fairs — with specific advice for building a collection locally in Slovenia and FVG.

---

## The Wine Shop

A good independent wine shop is your most valuable resource. The best ones are staffed by people who taste extensively and can guide you to things you'd never find on your own.

### How to Choose a Good Wine Shop

Signs of a good shop:
- Staff who taste the wines they sell and can describe them
- A selection beyond supermarket staples — indigenous grapes, small producers, regional wines
- Bottles stored horizontally or in a temperature-controlled space
- A distinct point of view — their selections reflect taste and judgment
- They ask questions before recommending

Signs to avoid:
- Bottles standing upright under fluorescent lights for years
- A selection of only well-known brands
- Staff who can't tell you anything about the wines

### The Conversation Strategy

Tell them:
1. **Budget** (be honest — "under €20" is more useful than vague)
2. **Occasion** (everyday dinner, special occasion, gift, aging)
3. **Style preferences** ("I like fresh, acidic whites" / "I want a red that isn't too tannic")
4. **What you've already enjoyed** (gives them a reference point)

A good shopkeeper will do the rest.

### Browsing Without Help

If you prefer to browse:
- The region is the first filter — you're building a mental map
- The producer is often more important than the appellation — research key producers in advance
- Vintage matters for wines you'll age — use the vintage chart reference sheet
- Compare prices across similar bottles to understand value benchmarks in that shop

## Restaurant Wine Lists

Restaurant lists are often marked up 3–4× the retail price. This changes the strategy.

### How to Navigate a List

**Start with the by-the-glass options.** They change often and represent what the sommelier is excited about. A restaurant with interesting BTG choices usually has an interesting full list.

**Look at the full list by region, not price.** Find regions you're curious about or know represent value.

**Look for producer names you recognize.** If you know *Movia* or *Vie di Romans*, seeing them on a list tells you the buyer has knowledge.

**Ask for help early:** "We're having the seafood pasta and the lamb — what would pair well with both?" This is a legitimate, common sommelier question.

**Find value:**
- Half bottles (*demi*) are often underpriced relative to full bottles
- Second-wine or lesser-known cuvées from good estates
- Lesser-known appellations on a classic list (a Bordeaux list's Côtes de Bourg is often cheaper than the Pauillac and equally good to drink)
- Grower Champagne vs. big house NV at similar price
- Slovenian or FVG wines on European restaurant lists — they are often underpriced because staff don't know them well

## Online Buying

### What Works Well Online

- Comparing prices for specific bottles you already know
- Accessing wines unavailable locally
- Reading producer/importer notes before buying
- Ordering mixed cases to explore a region

### Reliable Online Retailers (European focus)

- **Vinoptima** (Slovenia): Good Slovenian selection
- **Vinoteka Brda** (Slovenia/FVG): Direct from the Brda cooperative
- **Vino.si / Vinakoper**: Slovenian producers direct
- **Tannico.it** (Italy): Excellent Italian selection, ships EU-wide
- **Callmewine.com** (Italy): Broad Italian range, good FVG section
- **Wine-Searcher.com**: Price comparison tool; finds where to buy a specific bottle across all retailers
- **Vivino**: App with user ratings and where to buy; useful for price research but treat ratings with skepticism

### Wine-Searcher (wine-searcher.com)

The single most useful tool for wine buyers. Free to use; paid tier adds extras but the free version covers most needs.

**What it does:**
- Enter any producer + wine name → see current global market price range instantly
- Find every retailer stocking that bottle (local shops, EU-wide, global)
- Aggregated critic scores from multiple publications in one place
- Full producer profiles with their entire wine range and typical pricing
- Vintage notes on specific wines

**When to use it:**
- Before buying any bottle over €25 — verify you're not overpaying
- Before a winery visit — look up the producer, browse their full range and prices
- When you see an unfamiliar wine on a restaurant list — quick price check shows if the markup is reasonable
- Finding where to buy a specific wine locally when your usual shop doesn't stock it

**For FVG and Slovenian producers specifically:** Wine-Searcher has meaningfully better coverage of small Italian and Slovenian estates than crowd-sourced rating apps. Even for niche Collio or Brda producers, you'll typically find pricing and professional notes — making it more useful in this region than apps where obscure producers have too few reviews to mean anything.

## Wine Fairs and Tasting Events

### Local Events Worth Knowing

**Dnevi odprtih kleti (Open Cellar Days), Slovenia**  
Typically in late May/early June. Dozens of wineries across Primorska (Brda, Vipava, Kras) and other regions open their cellars for tastings and visits. Free or minimal entry per cellar. The best way to taste 20+ producers in a weekend and buy direct.

**Binkoštna tržnica vina, Goriška Brda**  
Pentecost wine market in Dobrovo, the heart of Brda. A key regional event.

**Vinomania, Ljubljana** (and similar events)  
Consumer wine fairs with broad Slovenian and imported selection.

**Vinitaly (Verona, Italy)**  
The world's largest wine fair. Trade-oriented but consumer days exist. Excellent for Italian wine discovery, including FVG producers.

**VinForum / Vinorum, Trieste**  
Regional events with strong FVG focus.

**Enolike (Gorizia / Nova Gorica area)**  
Cross-border events taking advantage of the unique Gorizia / Nova Gorica status as European Capital of Culture. Worth checking annually for FVG and Slovenian wine events.

### How to Use Wine Fairs

- Prioritize producers you've researched in advance (bring a list)
- Focus on tasting, not buying — you can order later
- Bring a notebook or a simple notes app (Vivino for scanning labels; for Slovenian/FVG producers use Wine-Searcher to research before attending)
- Ask producers: "What is your best vintage right now?" and "What bottle would you recommend for someone new to your wine?"
- Don't try to taste everything — you'll be useless after glass 15

## Buying Local: Slovenia and FVG

This section is specific to your location.

### Direct from Producers (Cellar Door)

Buying directly from the producer is:
- Cheapest (no retailer markup)
- Best for freshness (especially for current release)
- Gives you context (you see the cellar, the vineyards, meet the winemaker)
- Builds a relationship that can lead to allocation of limited wines

**Key producers with cellar door visits:**

**Goriška Brda / Collio:**
- **Movia** (Ceglo, Brda): Aleš Kristančič is often there; one of the most interesting cellar visits in Slovenia. Call ahead
- **Edi Simčič** (Gorizia): Family winery, welcoming
- **Klinec** (Medana, Brda): Small natural wine producer
- **Marjan Simčič** (Ceglo, Brda, IT side): Top Collio producer
- **Gravner** (Oslavia, Gorizia, IT): By appointment only, worth the effort
- **Radikon** (Oslavia, Gorizia, IT): Now run by the son Saša; by appointment
- **Borgo del Tiglio** (IT): By appointment

**Vipava Valley:**
- **Batič** (Šempas): Family winery, excellent range, easy visits
- **Burja Estate** (Planina): Top quality, natural wines
- **Guerila** (Ajdovščina): Innovative producer with great local food too
- **Tilia Estate**: Premium range

**Kras / Carso:**
- **Čotar** (Komen, SI): Traditional Vitovska and Teran specialist
- **Edi Kante** (Prepotto, Carso, IT): Benchmark Vitovska and Carso whites

### Local Wine Shops (Slovenia and Trieste area)

**Ljubljana:**
- **Vinska klet** (Old Town): Excellent Slovenian selection
- **Eno & Vino**: Knowledgeable staff, events
- **Dveri-Pax enoteka**: Certified Slovenian wines

**Nova Gorica / Gorizia area:**
- **Enoteca Regionale della Valtènesi** (varies location)
- **Wine shops in Gorizia**: Several near the town center with strong Collio selection

**Trieste:**
- **Enoteca Rabarbaro**: One of the best FVG selections
- **La Bottega del Vino**: Classic Triestine enoteca
- **Various osmize**: Traditional seasonal wine houses on the Karst (just outside Trieste). Serve home-produced wine with simple food. Announced with fresh pine branches on the gate. Ask a local, there's no fixed calendar

### What to Buy at Each Price Point

**Under €15:**
- Zelen or Pinela from Vipava Valley cooperatives (Vipavska Klet)
- Entry-level Rebula from Brda (Klet Brda co-op, well-made at this price)
- Friulano from Friuli Grave DOC (accessible and correct)
- Cviček PDO (the light Slovenian red blend — curiosity value)
- Teran (Kras): look for Čotar's basic range or Kras cooperative

**€15–30:**
- Ribolla Gialla from a serious Collio producer (Doro Princic, Primosic)
- Friulano from Collio or Colli Orientali (Ronco del Gelso, Dorigo)
- Malvasia Istriana from Carso (Kante, Zidarich)
- Schioppettino (Ronchi di Cialla)
- Rebula from Movia's basic range
- Batič Zaria blend (Vipava — excellent value)
- Burja Reddo (Vipava Valley red)

**€30–60:**
- Movia Lunar (orange Rebula — a benchmark skin-contact wine)
- Edi Simčič Kolos (Collio white blend — their top wine)
- Vitovska from Edi Kante or Zidarich
- Radikon Ribolla Gialla (mature vintages if available)
- Gravner Breg (entry-level by Gravner's standards)
- Borgo del Tiglio Collio Bianco

**€60+:**
- Gravner Ribolla Gialla Anfora (the iconic orange wine)
- Radikon Oslavje or Jakot (aged 10+ years)
- Movia Veliko Belo (top white blend)

## Building a Starter Collection

You don't need a cellar to start collecting wine. You need:
- A cool, dark spot (10–16°C consistently)
- Bottles lying on their sides
- A simple inventory list (spreadsheet or any notes app; CellarTracker works well for international wines but has sparse coverage of Slovenian producers)

### A Local Starter 12-Bottle Selection

| Wine | Why |
|------|-----|
| Klet Brda Rebula | Everyday white, value reference |
| Movia Ribolla | Serious white, age 2–3 years |
| Batič Zaria | Vipava Valley blend, versatile |
| Edi Simčič Rebula | Quality benchmark at this price |
| Radikon Ribolla (older vintage) | The orange wine experience |
| Gravner Breg | Introduction to amphora style |
| Čotar Vitovska | Kras white, unique mineral wine |
| Schioppettino di Prepotto | Top FVG red, food versatile |
| Movia Modri Pinot | Slovenian Pinot Noir, elegant |
| Burja Reddo | Vipava Valley red, natural, value |
| Collio Friulano (any serious producer) | Classic regional pairing wine |
| 1 wine from outside region | Reference point — e.g., white Burgundy or Riesling |

## Understanding Vintages When Buying

For wines you plan to drink within 1–2 years: vintage matters less.  
For wines you want to age or that are premium: vintage matters significantly.

The [vintage chart reference](../reference/vintage-chart.md) covers key regions including FVG/Slovenia.

General buying rule: **avoid the most recent vintage for powerful reds** (Barolo, Brunello, serious Bordeaux). They need time. Buy older vintages that are ready to drink, or buy young and cellar properly.

For FVG and Slovenian whites: most are best 2–5 years from vintage. Orange wines (Gravner, Radikon) are typically released 5–7 years after harvest and can age 15–25 years.
