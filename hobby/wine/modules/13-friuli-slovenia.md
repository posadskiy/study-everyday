# Module 13 — Friuli Venezia Giulia & Slovenia: Your Backyard Wines

This module is a deep-dive into the wine world closest to you. Friuli Venezia Giulia (FVG) and Slovenia share not just a border but the same hills, the same grapes, the same geological history, and in many cases the same winemaking philosophy. You have extraordinary access to some of Europe's most distinctive wines — this module gives you the context to fully appreciate them.

---

## Part 1: The Geography and History

### One Land, Two Countries

The border between Italy and Slovenia in the wine region is a historical accident, not a geographical one.

**Collio** (Italian) and **Goriška Brda** (Slovenian) are the same geological formation: a series of gentle hills of *ponca* (Eocene flysch — a sedimentary mix of marl and sandstone) rising from the Isonzo/Soča river valley to the Slovenian Karst plateau. The hills are continuous. The border runs through them arbitrarily.

Before World War I, this entire area was part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire — the County of Gorizia. Wines from both sides of today's border were traded together as "Görzer Wein" in Vienna and Trieste.

After WWI, the region was absorbed into Italy. After WWII, the border was drawn to separate Yugoslavia from Italy — splitting communities, families, and in some cases single estates. The Simčič family (Edi Simčič on the Slovenian side, Marjan Simčič on the Italian side) is perhaps the most famous example of a family split by the border, now running two highly regarded wineries on the same continuous hillside.

After Slovenian independence in 1991 and EU accession in 2004, the border effectively dissolved for practical purposes. Winemakers visit each other freely, cross-border collaborations are common, and the shared regional wine culture is increasingly recognized.

**Gorizia and Nova Gorica** were named the shared European Capital of Culture 2025 — the first cross-border pairing in the program's history. Wine is central to the region's cultural identity.

---

### The Isonzo/Soča Valley

The Soča (Isonzo in Italian) river runs from the Triglav massif in the Slovenian Alps through the Goriška plain and into the Adriatic. It is one of the most beautiful rivers in Europe — glacially cold, intensely turquoise.

The river moderates temperatures in the vineyards. Cold air from the Alps flows down at night, preserving acidity in the grapes despite warm sunny days. This thermal variation is one reason why the whites of Collio and Brda have both ripeness and freshness.

## Part 2: FVG Sub-Regions in Detail

### Collio DOC

**Location:** Between Gorizia and the Slovenian border  
**Soils:** *Ponca* (Eocene flysch — marl and sandstone); some alluvial soils near the valley  
**Altitude:** 50–250m  
**Climate:** Continental influence from the north; Adriatic moderating from the south; cold Bora wind from the Karst  

Collio is primarily a **white wine region** — the most distinguished in northeastern Italy. The ponca soils drain well and store heat, producing grapes of exceptional aromatic complexity. The combination of warm days and cool nights gives the wines both body and freshness.

**Key permitted white grapes:** Friulano, Ribolla Gialla, Malvasia Istriana, Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Bianco, Gewürztraminer, Traminer Aromatico, Chardonnay

**Collio DOC Bianco:** A blend-based designation (minimum 3 varieties, including Friulano, Ribolla, or Malvasia). Many top estates use this to produce their signature white blend.

**Key sub-villages:** Oslavia (center of the orange wine universe), Cormons (largest wine town, good cooperative), Capriva del Friuli, Cormòns, Dolegna del Collio

**Key producers:**
- **Gravner** (Oslavia): The most influential producer in the region's modern history. Only two wines: Ribolla Gialla and Breg (blended varieties). Both made in Georgian amphora (*qvevri*) with extended skin contact. Release at 6–7 years from harvest. Age 20+ years. Pilgrim destination for orange wine lovers
- **Radikon** (Oslavia): Stanko Radikon's family continue his work. Extended skin contact, zero sulfur, no filtration. The wines are sold in 500ml and 1L sizes (Stanko believed restaurant pour sizes wasted wine). Now made by son Saša
- **Borgo del Tiglio** (Brazzano): Nicola Manferrari makes some of the most precise and classical Collio whites. Reference-quality Friulano and Collio Bianco
- **Vie di Romans** (Mariano del Friuli, technically Friuli Isonzo but quality rivals Collio): Gianfranco Gallo makes benchmark Pinot Grigio, Chardonnay, and Sauvignon Blanc. A wine that challenges Burgundy Chardonnay on quality
- **Doro Princic**: Family producer making classically styled Collio whites
- **Marco Felluga / Russiz Superiore**: Mid-scale estate, reliable quality
- **Primosic**: Quality Collio and orange wine style under "Klin" label
- **Damijan Podversič**: Thoughtful, natural style producer in Gorizia; excellent Ribolla in various styles

**The Oslavia Subzone:**  
Oslavia deserves special mention. This small village (barely a hamlet) at the foot of the Goriška Brda hills was home to Gravner and Radikon — and through them became the origin point of the modern orange wine movement. Half a dozen other producers in the immediate vicinity also make skin-contact wine. When you visit, you can walk from cellar to cellar. Oslavia is now a protected designation for orange wine within Collio DOC (*Ribolla Gialla di Oslavia* DOC, still being formalized).

### Colli Orientali del Friuli (COF) DOC

**Location:** The hills east of Udine, running from Tarcento in the north to Cividale del Friuli and Prepotto in the south  
**Soils:** Flysch and Eocene marl (similar to Collio), plus some quartz-sandstone areas in the north  
**Altitude:** 100–500m

COF is larger and more varied than Collio. It produces both whites (similar to Collio in character) and, distinctively, indigenous red varieties that are found almost nowhere else.

**Key appellations within COF:**

- **Ramandolo DOCG:** In the northern hills. Made from Verduzzo Friulano (a local white grape) in a naturally sweet style. The wine has high acidity and dried apricot character — one of Italy's more unusual dessert wines
- **Rosazzo DOCG:** A historic vineyard in the south of COF. Focused on Friulano (here the Friulano from Rosazzo is considered some of the finest in the region)
- **Prepotto:** The heartland of Schioppettino. A sub-zone name increasingly seen on labels

**Key indigenous reds of COF:**
- **Schioppettino:** The most celebrated. Nearly extinct in the 1970s. A group of Prepotto producers petitioned to have it reinstated in the DOC (it had been de-listed). Since its reinstatement, quality has risen steadily. Best examples: *Ronchi di Cialla*, *Petrussa*, *Specogna*
- **Pignolo:** Rare, powerful, needs 10+ years. Best: *Roberto Scubla*, *Moschioni*, *Abbazia di Rosazzo*
- **Tazzelenghe:** The "tongue-cutter." Very high acid, medium body. A food wine par excellence
- **Verduzzo Friulano:** White/amber grape for both dry and sweet wines

**Key producers:**
- **Miani** (Buttrio): Perhaps COF's most prestigious white wine producer. Extraordinary Friulano and Malvasia at collector prices
- **Ronchi di Cialla**: Historic estate; revived Schioppettino and Refosco
- **Livio Felluga**: One of FVG's large estates; the "Terre Alte" blend is a benchmark
- **Dorigo**: Good range across COF varieties
- **Specogna**: Excellent Schioppettino and Ribolla
- **Roberto Scubla**: Notable Pignolo and Picolit
- **Giovanni Dri (Il Roncat)**: Ramandolo specialist

---

### Friuli Isonzo DOC

**Location:** The flat alluvial plain of the Isonzo/Soča river, south of Gorizia  
**Soils:** River gravel and alluvial deposits — minerally, mineral-rich  
**Altitude:** 10–40m (very flat)

Despite the flat terrain (unusual for quality wine), the mineral-rich alluvial soils and cool river influence produce wines of surprising precision. This is where **Pinot Grigio at its finest** is made — not the thin, industrial versions of Veneto, but the version that reminds you why the grape was ever considered interesting.

**Key grapes:** Pinot Grigio (arguably Italy's best here), Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Friulano, Malvasia

**Key producer: Vie di Romans** — Gianfranco Gallo's estate here produces wines that compete with quality far above their DOC tier. The Pinot Grigio "Dessimis" and the Chardonnay "Ciampagnis Vieris" are benchmarks.

### Carso / Kras DOC

**Location:** The limestone plateau extending from Trieste northeast into Slovenia  
**Soils:** Terra rossa — red iron-rich clay over fractured Cretaceous limestone  
**Altitude:** 200–400m  
**Microclimate:** Exposed to the fierce **Bora wind** (the "Burja" in Slovenian) — a katabatic wind descending from the north that can reach 170 km/h. It reduces humidity, prevents fungal disease, and concentrates flavors

The Karst (Carso/Kras) is geologically and stylistically distinct from Collio and COF. The limestone base and red soils produce wines with extreme minerality, salinity, and structure that recall no other region.

**Key grapes:**

*Whites:*
- **Vitovska:** The signature white of the Carso. Ancient variety found almost nowhere else. Mineral, saline, flint, citrus. Profound example of terroir-specific character. See Module 2.
- **Malvasia Istriana:** Aromatic, gentle, floral. At its best on the Carso.

*Reds:*
- **Terrano:** The local biotype of Refosco. Intensely dark, very high tannins and acidity, enormous polyphenol content. Almost purple-black. One of the most unusual red wines in Europe. Traditionally considered a "health wine" by locals (antioxidants, etc.). Pairs specifically with the fatty, cured meats of Karst cuisine

**Key producers:**
- **Edi Kante** (Prepotto, Carso): The most internationally known Carso producer. Benchmark Vitovska and Carso white
- **Zidarich** (Duino-Aurisina): Exceptional Vitovska and Malvasia; natural farming
- **Skerk** (Aurisina): Precise, mineral whites
- **Parovel**: Long-established family estate
- **Lupinc**: Small, artisanal Carso producer

**The Osmiza tradition:** On the Carso around Trieste, traditional farmers have the right to sell their own wine directly from their property for short seasonal periods (historically linked to the *Osmiza* law under the Habsburg). When wine is available, a pine branch (šopek) is hung at the gate. There is no menu, no booking, and often no set schedule. Simple food (charcuterie, cheese, hard-boiled eggs, bread) accompanies the home wine. This is one of the great wine experiences of Europe — authentic, local, inexpensive.

---

### Friuli Grave DOC

**Location:** The large flat zone west of Udine and Pordenone, crossing into the Grave river gravel bed  
**Style:** High-volume, accessible, commercial  
**Significance:** Large-production DOC accounting for much of FVG's volume. Good value at entry level — Pinot Grigio, Merlot, Cabernet at affordable prices. Not where the great wines are, but where everyday drinking wines are.

## Part 3: Slovenian Wine Regions in Detail

### Primorska — The Wine Capital

Primorska (literally "the coastal region") is Slovenia's most prestigious wine region. It contains four sub-regions that share the geological and climatic characteristics of FVG.

```
Primorska
├── Goriška Brda          ← Collio's Slovenian twin
├── Vipavska Dolina       ← Valley specializing in indigenous grapes
├── Kras                  ← Karst limestone; Vitovska and Teran
└── Slovenska Istra       ← Coastal; Malvasia Istriana; Refosco
```

---

### Goriška Brda

**Location:** The rolling flysch hills between Gorizia and Nova Gorica  
**Soils:** Ponca (same Eocene flysch as Collio — identical geology)  
**Altitude:** 80–400m  
**Climate:** Mediterranean influence moderated by Alpine cold nights

Brda and Collio are the same geological unit separated by the political border. The wines are similarly styled. The main distinctions are regulatory (different DOC/PDO systems), not qualitative.

**Key grapes:** Rebula (= Ribolla Gialla), Friulano (here called Jakot), Malvasia, Pinot Grigio, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc; Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon, Pinot Noir for reds

**Key producers:**

- **Movia** (Ceglo): Aleš Kristančič is one of Slovenia's most charismatic winemakers. His philosophy: minimal intervention, wild fermentation, no added sulfur. Flagship wines include *Lunar* (unfiltered sparkling Rebula made without disgorgement) and *Veliko Belo* (the top white blend). Movia hosts one of the most theatrical disgorgement ceremonies in wine — the *en plein air* disgorgement, where bottles are opened underwater and the sediment is expelled in the stream
- **Edi Simčič** (Goriška Brda): Son of Teodor Simčič. Makes both conventional and skin-contact wines. *Kolos* is the flagship white blend — consistently excellent
- **Klinec** (Medana): Aleš Klinec makes genuinely natural wine — wild fermentation, no added sulfur, skin contact. One of the most interesting small producers in the region
- **Tilia Estate**: Premium range; more polished, internationally styled
- **Bjana** (Šmartno): Focusing on sparkling wine from Brda; unusual positioning
- **Klet Brda** (co-operative): The Brda cooperative, one of the largest wine producers in Slovenia. Reliable quality at affordable prices. The *Bagueri* and *Quercus* ranges offer good entry-level Rebula and blends

**Cross-border note — Marjan Simčič (IT):** Edi Simčič's cousin on the Italian side of the same hillside. Makes extremely precise, technically immaculate Collio wines. The comparison between the two estates is fascinating — same hillside, same family origin, different country, somewhat different styles.

---

### Vipavska Dolina (Vipava Valley)

**Location:** The valley of the Vipava River, between Nova Gorica and Ljubljana  
**Soils:** Limestone, alluvial gravel, clay-marl  
**Altitude:** 100–400m  
**Climate:** Protected from the Bora by the Trnovo Forest plateau to the north; strong Bora influence on the valley floor and open slopes

The Vipava Valley is Slovenia's most exciting wine region for indigenous grape variety preservation and natural wine innovation.

**What makes it unique:**
- Home to **Zelen** and **Pinela** — two white grapes grown almost exclusively here, nowhere else in the world
- Warm dry climate with excellent Bora ventilation (low disease pressure = organic farming easy)
- Strong community of natural winemakers
- Less internationally known than Brda, meaning better value

**Key grapes:** Zelen, Pinela (indigenous whites), Malvasia, Rebula, Chardonnay, Vitovska; Modra Frankinja (Blaufränkisch), Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Pinot Noir for reds

**Key producers:**
- **Batič** (Šempas): One of the most important wine families in Slovenia. Ivan Batič pioneered biodynamic viticulture in Slovenia. The *Zaria* blend (red) and *Angel* range are benchmarks. Family estate now run by his son Miha. Open for visits
- **Burja Estate** (Planina pod Golico): Primož Lavrenčič makes some of Slovenia's most serious natural wines. Aged in large neutral oak. The *Reddo* (red) and *Burja Bela* (white) are acclaimed
- **Guerila** (Ajdovščina): Run by Uroš Valenčič. More innovative and experimental than most — also has a restaurant/agritourism. One of the best places to taste the valley's diversity
- **Sutor**: Small producer; excellent Zelen and Pinela
- **Dolfo (Krasno)**: Solid producer, good everyday range
- **Vipavska Klet (co-operative)**: The valley cooperative. Makes large volumes; the value tier for Zelen and Pinela
- **Scurek**: Well-regarded; both conventional and skin-contact styles

---

### Kras (Karst)

**Location:** The high limestone plateau south of the Vipava Valley, extending to the coast and across into Italy (Carso)  
**Soils:** Terra rossa over fractured Cretaceous limestone  
**Altitude:** 200–450m

The Slovenian Kras is the continuation of the Italian Carso plateau. The wine tradition is ancient — Teran (the local Refosco biotype) has been documented here for centuries. Karst farmers historically grew wine for their own consumption and sold surplus from their property — the origin of the osmiza tradition.

**Key grapes:**
- **Vitovska** (white): Same as Italian Carso — mineral, saline, unique
- **Teran** (red): Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) in Slovenia. A long-running dispute with Italy (who wanted to use the name for a different wine) was resolved in Slovenia's favor. Teran is dense, tannic, very high in anthocyanins and polyphenols — intensely purple. The locals have traditionally drunk it for health

**Key producers:**
- **Čotar** (Komen): Branko Čotar and son Vasja. The most dedicated traditionalists of the Kras — making Vitovska and Teran with minimal intervention for decades before it was fashionable. Essential visit
- **Štemberger** (Kobdilj): Excellent Vitovska
- **Renčel**: Good Teran; traditional style
- **Vino Kras (co-operative)**: Large volume; introduces the region's grapes at accessible prices

---

### Slovenska Istra (Slovenian Istria)

**Location:** The Slovenian coastal strip south of Koper/Capodistria  
**Soils:** Limestone and flysch  
**Climate:** Mediterranean — warmest wine zone in Slovenia  

**Key grapes:** Malvasia Istriana (the regional white specialty), Refosco, Merlot, Cabernet

**Key producers:**
- **Vinakoper (co-operative)**: The dominant Istra producer. *Refosk* and *Malvazija* are the flagship wines. Good quality at accessible prices — often the introduction to Istra wine for tourists
- **Štokelj**: Small family producer; quality Malvasia
- **Edi Keber**: Not to be confused with Edi Kante in Carso — this is a Collio producer on the Italian side

---

### Podravje (Drava River Wine Region)

**Location:** Northeast Slovenia, along the Drava River; sub-regions include Štajerska Slovenija and Prekmurje  
**Climate:** Continental — cold winters, warm summers; frost risk  

Often overlooked by wine tourists focused on Primorska, Podravje has its own distinct character:

- **Šipon (Furmint):** The same grape as Hungary's Tokaji Furmint. Dry, high acidity, mineral. Excellent in Štajerska
- **Renski Rizling (Rhine Riesling):** Crisp, mineral Riesling in cool styles
- **Laški Rizling (Welschriesling):** Different and less distinguished than true Riesling; high volume; easy drinking
- **Rumeni Muškat (Yellow Muscat):** Aromatic, perfumed whites
- **Modra Frankinja (Blaufränkisch):** The best reds of Podravje

**Key producers:**
- **Dveri-Pax**: Bio-certified; solid range; widely distributed in Slovenia
- **Pullus**: Good-quality Štajerska whites; reliably made
- **Ptujska klet**: Historic cellar; Šipon specialist
- **Kupljen**: Excellent small producer in Haloze area
- **Steyer**: Good Šipon

---

### Posavje (Sava River Wine Region)

**Location:** South-central Slovenia along the Sava River  
**Wines:** Lighter, simpler; traditional for the Cviček PDO

**Cviček PDO:** A unique protected designation. A blend of red and white grapes (Žametovka, Modra Frankinja, Refosco, Laški Rizling, Welschriesling, others) producing a very light (8.5–10% alcohol), high-acid, pale ruby wine. It is a historically important regional tradition — very affordable, extremely local, and unlike anything else. Best drunk young, with simple food.

## Part 4: The Orange Wine Revolution

### The Story of Oslavia

In 1987, Joško Gravner of Oslavia began to question the direction of modern Italian winemaking. He had embraced technology — stainless steel, temperature control, new barriques — and made technically perfect wines. But they felt empty to him. The village's old-timers remembered that wine was made differently.

In 1997, Gravner traveled to California and was disappointed. He then visited Georgia — and found ancient winemaking traditions using large clay vessels (*qvevri*) buried in the earth, with extended skin maceration lasting months. No temperature control. Wild yeast. No sulfur. The wine was different from anything in Western Europe.

He returned to Oslavia and began experimenting: white grapes left on skins for weeks, then months. Fermentation in clay amphora. Results were initially controversial — the Italian wine press was baffled. Over the following decade, the wines matured into something extraordinary: amber-colored, tannic, structured whites with 20+ years of aging potential, unlike anything in Italy.

Stanko Radikon, a neighbor and friend, was following a parallel path. His wines became as influential as Gravner's, though stylistically distinct: slightly shorter skin contact, but equally committed to the minimal-intervention philosophy. He pioneered the 500ml and 1L bottle formats.

### The Ripple Effect

By the mid-2000s, a generation of young winemakers in Brda, the Vipava Valley, the Kras, and the Collio were visiting Gravner and Radikon and rethinking their approach. Names like Movia (Aleš Kristančič), Klinec, Čotar, Batič, and eventually producers across the natural wine world were making skin-contact wine.

The term "orange wine" was coined by British wine importer David Harvey in 2004 to describe the color category. Simon J. Woolf's writing and his 2018 book *Amber Revolution* brought the story to a global audience.

Today, skin-contact white wine is made on every continent. Its cultural epicenter remains the hills around Gorizia.

## Part 6: Practical Local Guide

### Wine Road Trips

**Route 1: Goriška Brda Wine Road (1 day)**
Base: Dobrovo or Šmartno  
Start at Klet Brda cooperative for orientation, then drive the Brda hills road through Medana (Klinec), Ceglo (Movia, Marjan Simčič), ending at Edi Simčič. The views over the Soča valley and the Julian Alps are exceptional. Villages like Šmartno (a medieval walled village) are worth a stop.

**Route 2: Oslavia and Collio Cross-Border (half day)**  
Start in Gorizia old town. Cross into Nova Gorica. Drive to Oslavia (3 km from Gorizia). Visit Gravner (by appointment) or Radikon (by appointment). Cross back to the Italian side and visit Borgo del Tiglio or Primosic. Lunch in one of Cormons' restaurants (Trattoria al Cacciatore is excellent).

**Route 3: Vipava Valley (1 day)**  
Drive the V3 wine road. Stop at Batič (Šempas), Guerila (Ajdovščina), Burja (Planina). Lunch at Guerila's restaurant. The valley is dramatic — steep slopes, the Bora visible in the pine forests that lean permanently northwest.

**Route 4: Karst / Kras (afternoon)**  
Trieste as base. Drive up to the Karst plateau. Find an osmiza (ask locals, or check osmize.com for current openings). Visit Čotar in Komen. Walk the Lipica area.

## Self-Quiz

1. Why are Collio (IT) and Goriška Brda (SI) considered the same wine region by geography?
2. What is *ponca* and how does it affect wine character?
3. Who were the two winemakers in Oslavia who started the modern orange wine movement, and what inspired them?
4. What is the osmiza tradition and what is the signal that one is open?
5. What makes the Vipava Valley unique among Slovenian wine regions?
6. Name three important producers in Goriška Brda and one in the Vipava Valley.
7. What is Teran and why is it protected as a PDO?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. They are the same continuous geological formation — a series of hills made of *ponca* (Eocene flysch) rising from the Soča/Isonzo valley. The border that separates them is a political line drawn after WWII, not a natural one. Before 1945, the entire area was unified first under Austria-Hungary, then Italy. The hills, soils, climate, and grape varieties are identical on both sides.

2. Ponca is Eocene flysch — a sedimentary rock mix of marl (calcium carbonate-rich clay) and sandstone, deposited as ancient seabed. It drains well, retains warmth, and breaks down slowly into a mineral-rich soil. The result is wines with notable aromatics, good acidity, and mineral precision — the characteristic "Collio/Brda style" of white wine.

3. Joško Gravner and Stanko Radikon. Gravner was inspired by a visit to Georgia where he encountered ancient *qvevri* (clay amphora) winemaking with months of skin contact. He returned to Oslavia in the late 1990s and began applying these methods to Ribolla Gialla. Radikon followed a parallel path independently, arriving at similar conclusions.

4. An osmiza is a seasonal farm wine house on the Karst plateau around Trieste, where farmers can sell their own wine directly for short periods (a right dating to the Habsburg era). Simple food accompanies the wine. The signal that one is open is a fresh pine branch (*šopek*) hung at the gate. No booking, no fixed schedule.

5. It is home to Zelen and Pinela — two white grape varieties grown almost exclusively here and almost nowhere else on earth. The Bora wind (Burja) from the north ventilates the valley, reducing disease pressure and making organic farming relatively easy. A strong community of natural winemakers (Batič, Burja, Guerila, Sutor) has made it Slovenia's most exciting region for indigenous variety preservation.

6. Brda: Movia, Edi Simčič, Klinec (and many others: Tilia, Bjana, Klet Brda co-op). Vipava Valley: Batič (also Burja Estate, Guerila, Sutor).

7. Teran is a red wine from the Kras (Karst), made from a local biotype of Refosco. It is very dense, almost purple-black, with exceptionally high tannins, very high acidity, and very high polyphenol/antioxidant content. Slovenia fought a long EU dispute to protect the name — Italy wanted to use "Teran" for a different wine, but Slovenia prevailed. Teran PDO now legally belongs exclusively to the Slovenian Kras region.

</details>
