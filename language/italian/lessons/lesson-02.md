## 1) Gender (very basic rules)

Many Italian nouns end in:
- **-o** → usually **masculine**: *ragazzo* (boy), *libro* (book)
- **-a** → usually **feminine**: *ragazza* (girl), *casa* (house)
- **-e** → can be masculine or feminine: *studente* (male student), *stazione* (station)

Don’t worry about exceptions yet—learn words with their article.

## 2) Indefinite articles (a / an)

- **un** + masculine (most cases): *un libro*  
- **uno** + masculine before **s+consonant**, **z**, **gn**, **ps**: *uno studente*, *uno zaino*  
- **una** + feminine: *una casa*  
- **un’** + feminine before a vowel: *un’amica*

## 3) Definite articles (the)

- **il** + masculine (simple start): *il libro*  
- **lo** + masculine before **s+consonant**, **z**, **gn**, **ps**: *lo studente*, *lo zaino*  
- **la** + feminine: *la casa*  
- **l’** + before a vowel (masc/fem): *l’amico*, *l’amica*

## 4) Plurals (starter patterns)

- **-o → -i**: *libro → libri*
- **-a → -e**: *casa → case*
- **-e → -i**: *studente → studenti*, *stazione → stazioni*

## Vocabulary (learn with article)

- **il libro** — the book
- **la casa** — the house
- **lo studente / la studentessa** — the student (m/f)
- **uno zaino** — a backpack
- **un’amica** — a (female) friend
- **l’amico** — the (male) friend
