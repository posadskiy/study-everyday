## Part 1 — Past tense and word order

### Past tense: the whole system

Slovenian past tense is not one word. It is a **compound tense**:

**present of *biti*** + **l-participle**

| Person | Auxiliary | Example with *iti* | Meaning |
|--------|-----------|--------------------|---------|
| jaz | sem | *sem šel / šla* | I went |
| ti | si | *si šel / šla* | you went |
| on / ona / ono | je | *je šel / šla / šlo* | he / she / it went |
| midva / midve | sva | *sva šla / šli* | we two went |
| vidva / vidve | sta | *sta šla / šli* | you two went |
| onadva / onidve | sta | *sta šla / šli* | they two went |
| mi / me | smo | *smo šli / šle* | we went |
| vi / ve | ste | *ste šli / šle* | you went |
| oni / one | so | *so šli / šle* | they went |

The **auxiliary** shows person and number. The **l-participle** shows gender and number.

| Subject | Correct | Why |
|---------|---------|-----|
| *Marko* | *Marko je šel.* | masculine singular |
| *Ana* | *Ana je šla.* | feminine singular |
| *Marko in Ana* | *Marko in Ana sta šla.* | dual, mixed/masculine form |
| *Ana in Maja* | *Ana in Maja sta šli.* | dual feminine |
| *študenti* | *Študenti so šli.* | plural masculine |
| *študentke* | *Študentke so šle.* | plural feminine |

Russian reference: Russian past tense has gender (*я пошёл / я пошла*), but it does **not** use a present-tense auxiliary. Slovenian does:

| Russian | Slovenian |
|---------|-----------|
| *я пошёл* | *sem šel* / *jaz sem šel* |
| *она была дома* | *je bila doma* / *ona je bila doma* |
| *мы не имели времени* | *nismo imeli časa* |

Do not drop the auxiliary in standard Slovenian. *Šel v šolo* is fragmentary; *sem šel v šolo* is the normal sentence.

### Negative past

Negative past uses the negative auxiliary:

| Positive | Negative |
|----------|----------|
| *sem šel* | *nisem šel* |
| *si bila* | *nisi bila* |
| *je imel* | *ni imel* |
| *sva šla* | *nisva šla* |
| *sta prišla* | *nista prišla* |
| *smo imeli* | *nismo imeli* |
| *ste videli* | *niste videli* |
| *so bili* | *niso bili* |

After negation, the object often becomes **genitive**:

- *Imam čas.* → *Nimam časa.*
- *Videl sem film.* → *Nisem videl filma.*
- *Pili smo vino.* → *Nismo pili vina.*

This is close to Russian: *нет времени*, *не видел фильма*. It is one of the useful transfers from Russian.

### Word order: what “second position” really means

Slovenian has flexible word order for emphasis, but small unstressed words are strict. The auxiliary (*sem, si, je, sva, sta, smo, ste, so*) is a **clitic**. It usually wants the **second position** in the clause.

Second position means: after the first meaningful block, not always after one written word.

| First block | Auxiliary | Rest |
|-------------|-----------|------|
| *Včeraj* | *sem* | *šel v trgovino.* |
| *V Ljubljani* | *sem* | *bil včeraj.* |
| *Moja prijateljica Ana* | *je* | *prišla pozno.* |
| *Ko sem imel čas,* | *sem* | *bral slovensko.* |

Normal patterns:

- *Včeraj sem šel v šolo.*
- *Jaz sem šel v šolo.*
- *V šolo sem šel včeraj.*
- *Marko in Ana sta šla v kino.*
- *Kje si bil včeraj?*
- *A si že jedel?*

Bad Russian-style patterns:

- ✗ *Včeraj šel sem v šolo.*
- ✗ *Jaz šel sem v šolo.*
- ✗ *Kje bil si včeraj?*
- ✗ *Ana bila je doma.*

### Is *šel sem* always wrong?

No. This is why the rule needs nuance.

In neutral sentences, especially after a fronted time/place/topic, use auxiliary early:

- *Včeraj sem šel domov.*
- *Potem sem kupil kruh.*
- *V parku sva sedela na klopi.*

But participle-first order can appear when the participle itself is emphasized, in short answers, corrections, or literary/emotional style:

- *Šel sem, ampak nisem nič kupil.* — I did go, but I bought nothing.
- *Bil sem tam!* — I was there!
- *Videla sem ga, ne slišala.* — I saw him, not heard him.

For your active A2→B1 speech, use the safe default: **first block + auxiliary + participle**.

### Questions

There are two common question types.

With a question word:

- *Kje si bil?* — Where were you?
- *Kam je šla?* — Where did she go?
- *Kaj sta kupila?* — What did the two of them buy?
- *Kdaj ste prišli?* — When did you arrive?

With *a/ali*:

- *A si že jedel?* — Have you eaten already?
- *Ali ste bili doma?* — Were you at home?
- *A sta vidva videla film?* — Did you two see the film?

In questions, the auxiliary still stays early. Do not move it after the participle.

### Multiple clitics

Slovenian often stacks small words after the first block:

- *Včeraj sem ga videl.* — Yesterday I saw him.
- *Ana mi je povedala novico.* — Ana told me the news.
- *Kje si ga videl?* — Where did you see him?
- *Nisem ga videl.* — I did not see him.

For now, learn the feel: first meaningful block, then small grammar words, then the main verb/rest.

### Subjects are optional

Because the auxiliary already tells person, Slovenian often drops the subject:

- *Sem šel v trgovino.* = I went to the store.
- *Sva bila doma.* = We two were at home.
- *Nismo imeli časa.* = We did not have time.

Use the subject when you contrast or clarify:

- *Jaz sem šel v trgovino, Ana pa je šla v knjižnico.*
- *Midva sva bila doma, oni pa so šli ven.*

### Core examples

1. *Včeraj sem šel v trgovino in kupil kruh.* — Yesterday I went to the store and bought bread.
2. *Ana je bila v knjižnici, Marko pa je bil doma.* — Ana was in the library, while Marko was at home.
3. *Marko in Ana sta sedela na klopi.* — Marko and Ana were sitting on a bench.
4. *Nismo imeli časa, zato nismo gledali filma.* — We did not have time, so we did not watch the film.
5. *Kje si bila včeraj?* — Where were you yesterday?
6. *A sta vidva že jedla?* — Have you two eaten already?

## Part 2 — The six cases (overview)

### Concept

Slovenian has **6 cases** — same count as Russian, different endings and questions.

| # | Slovenian | Russian | Question (SI) | Question (RU) | Typical role |
|---|-----------|---------|---------------|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | **imenovalnik** | **именительный** | Kdo? Kaj? | Кто? Что? | subject |
| 2 | **rodilnik** | **родительный** | Koga? Česa? | Кого? Чего? | possession, negation, quantity |
| 3 | **dajalnik** | **дательный** | Komu? Čemu? | Кому? Чему? | indirect object |
| 4 | **tožilnik** | **винительный** | Koga? Kaj? | Кого? Что? | direct object, direction |
| 5 | **mestnik** | **предложный** | O kom? O čem? | О ком? О чём? | location (*with* preposition) |
| 6 | **orodnik** | **творительный** | S kom? S čim? | С кем? С чем? | means, accompaniment |

### Russian reference — what transfers

| Works similarly | Different in Slovenian |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| Genitive after negation: *нет времени* → *nimam časa* | Locative **only with a preposition** (*v šoli*, not *šoli* alone) |
| Dative experiencer: *мне холодно* → *hladno mi je* | **Dual** number for exactly two |
| Instrumental *с*: *с другом* → *s prijateljem* | *v* + locative (where) vs *v* + accusative (where to) |
| Six cases feel familiar | Endings differ — do not copy Russian forms |

Learn cases as **preposition + ending chunks**: *v šoli*, *v šolo*, *nimam časa*, *s prijateljem*.

### Endings you need for practice

Cases are not just names — you need **endings**. For Module 01, learn three singular patterns; print the full tables from **[Appendix B — Case endings (A2)](../reference/case-endings-a2.md)** and use them for every §2 drill.

| Pattern | Model | rodilnik | tožilnik | mestnik | orodnik |
|---------|-------|----------|----------|---------|---------|
| feminine *-a* | *miza* | mize | mizo | mizi | mizo |
| masc. consonant | *prijatelj* | prijatelja | prijatelja¹ | prijatelju | prijateljem |
| neuter *-o* | *mesto* | mesta | mesto | mestu | mestom |

¹ Animate masculine (people, animals): accusative = genitive (*vidim psa*). Inanimate: accusative = nominative (*vidim vlak*).

**Drill loop:** (1) name the case from context → (2) pick the noun pattern → (3) read the ending from Appendix B → (4) say the phrase aloud.

### Examples

1. *Vidim mizo.* (acc) — I see the table. *miza* → tožilnik *-o*
2. *Nimam časa.* (gen) — I don't have time. *čas* → rodilnik *-a*
3. *Dam ti knjigo.* (dat) — I give you the book. *knjiga* → dajalnik *-i*
4. *Sem v šoli.* (loc) — I'm at school. *šola* → mestnik *-i*
5. *Pijem s prijateljem.* (ins) — I'm drinking with a friend. *prijatelj* → orodnik *-em*

## Part 3 — Locative vs accusative with *v* and *na*

### Concept

A very common learner mistake: confusing **where** (*kje*) with **where to** (*kam*).

| Meaning | Case | Question | Example |
|---------|------|----------|---------|
| Location | **Locative** (5th) | **Kje?** (where) | *Sem **v šoli**.* |
| Direction | **Accusative** (4th) | **Kam?** (where to) | *Grem **v šolo**.* |

Same with **na**:

| Location | Direction |
|----------|-----------|
| *Sem **na trgu**.* | *Grem **na trg**.* |
| *Sem **na morju**.* | *Grem **na morje**.* |
| *Sem **na univerzi**.* | *Grem **na univerzo**.* |

### Russian reference

| Russian | Slovenian |
|---------|-----------|
| *я **в** школ**е*** (locative/prep) | *sem **v** šol**i*** |
| *я иду **в** школ**у*** (acc) | *grem **v** šol**o*** |
| *где?* → locative | *kje?* → locative |
| *куда?* → accusative | *kam?* → accusative |

**Trap from Russian:** do not use locative for direction (*grem v šoli* ✗).

**Adverbs — no case change:** *domov* (home), *ven* (outside), *noter* (inside).

### Real speech

- *Kam greš?* — *V trgovino.*
- *Kje si?* — *V trgovini.*
- *Greš v trgovino?* — casual, no *kam*

### Examples

1. *Otrok je v šoli.* — The child is at school.
2. *Otrok gre v šolo.* — The child is going to school.
3. *Knjiga je na mizi.* — The book is on the table.
4. *Daj knjigo na mizo.* — Put the book on the table.
5. *Živim v Ljubljani, vikend pa sem na morju.* — I live in Ljubljana, on weekends I'm at the sea.

## Part 4 — Dual (dvojina)

### Concept

For **exactly two** people or things, Slovenian uses **dual** — Russian does **not** have this. You must learn it separately.

| Number | Pronouns | Past auxiliary |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| 1 | *jaz* | *sem* |
| 2 | *midva/midve*, *vidva/vidve*, *onadva/onidve* | ***sva***, ***sta*** |
| 3+ | *mi*, *vi*, *oni* | *smo*, *ste*, *so* |

**Past tense = dual auxiliary + l-participle** (auxiliary is mandatory):

| | Example |
|---|---------|
| we two | *Midva **sva** šla.* |
| you two | *Vidva **sta** prišla?* |
| they two | *Onadva **sta** videla.* |

Common mistake: *viste prišli* → ***Vidva sta prišla.***

**midva** = two men or mixed · **midve** = two women

### Russian reference

No dual in Russian — you use plural (*мы двое пошли*) where Slovenian requires dual forms (*midva sva šla*). Using plural for two people (*mi smo šli* when only two) sounds wrong in Slovenian.

### Real speech

- *Kje sta vidva?* — Where are you two?
- *Sva šla v kino.* — (We two) went to the cinema. (subject dropped)
- *A sta lačna?* — Are you two hungry?

### Examples

1. *Midva sva se spoznala lani.* — We two met last year.
2. *Vidva sta prišla prepozno.* — You two came too late.
3. *Onadva sta delala včeraj.* — Those two were working yesterday.

### Grammar traps (patterns — learn here)

| Wrong (from Russian) | Correct Slovenian |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| *šel sem* (participle + auxiliary) | ***sem šel*** |
| plural for exactly two people | ***midva sva*** + dual verb |

Russian **case names** match the system (see Part 2). Russian **case endings** do not.

### Examples (from appendix)

1. *Novice so bile dobre.* — The news was good. (*novice*, not *vest*)
2. *Grad stoji na hribu.* — The castle stands on the hill. (*grad* ≠ city)
3. *Stol stoji ob mizi.* — The chair is by the table. (*stol* ≠ RU *стол*)
4. *Zdaj grem v šolo.* — Now I'm going to school.
